## 1. SANBORN'S SIGNATURE STYLE & TECHNIQUES

### Artistic Philosophy
- **Quote:** "The fact that my earlier work didn't include cryptography doesn't mean it excluded things dealing with secrecy. The work that happened before the CIA, and (was) the reason I was chosen (to do the CIA piece), dealt with invisible forces -- albeit natural invisible forces."
- **Mission:** Make the invisible visible
- **Approach:** "Content is sometimes more important than the meaning of work"
- **Training:** Combines art history, sociology, archaeology, and sculpture (NOT mathematics)

### Non-Mathematical Background
- Sanborn is **mathematically illiterate** (his own words)
- Never studied codes in youth
- Heavily influenced by Minimalism and early Environmentalism
- **Result:** Designed ciphers that prioritize **artistic storytelling over pure mathematical elegance**

### Signature Materials & Methods
- **Primary Medium:** Copper sculptures (chosen for permanence and aesthetic appeal)
- **Text Processing:**
  - Modern: Computer layout + water jet cutting (post-Kryptos works)
  - Kryptos: Hand-cut copper with jigsaw (2 years, 9 jigsaws, 900 blades, 12 assistants)
  - Hand-drilled each letter 4 times

- **Unusual Materials Used in Works:**
  - Pulped CIA documents
  - Petrified trees
  - Shadows
  - Lightning
  - Artificially-aged sandstone
  - Wave machines
  - Egyptian mummified remains
  - Original scientific laboratory objects

### Key Artistic Techniques
- **Inscription Method:** Text cut into copper panels with visible patina
- **Layering:** Multiple information levels (visible text + hidden structure + sculptural form)
- **Thematic Integration:** Text content relates directly to sculpture's location/mission
- **Site-Specific Design:** "Work I do that is site-specific work is based on the mission of the agency I'm doing it for"

**⚠️ IMPLICATION FOR K4:** The encryption may incorporate **site-specific geographic elements, physical landmarks, or real-world coordinates** rather than purely mathematical keys.

## 2. CIPHER METHODS USED IN SANBORN'S OTHER WORKS

### Kryptos Sculpture (CIA Headquarters, 1990)
**Four encrypted passages:**
- **K1:** Simple substitution (Palimpsest cipher - solved 1998)
- **K2:** Vigenère cipher (solved 1998)
- **K3:** Transposition cipher (solved 1998)
- **K4:** Complex unknown method (97 characters, unsolved until 2025)

**Ed Scheidt (CIA cryptographer) noted:** "There are four different processes. Two of them are similar and the other two are different things."

### Antipodes (1997) - Hirshhorn Museum, Washington DC
- **Design:** Two copper curved pieces with petrified wood separator
- **Content:** Dual encoding (CIA operations vs. KGB operations)
- **Methods:**
  - English side repeats Kryptos text with alternations
  - Cyrillic side includes coded Russian text
- **Theme:** "Covert Balance" between opposing intelligence agencies
- **Status:** Cyrillic side solved (September 2003)

**KEY FINDING:** Sanborn repeats and modifies ciphertext across works, suggesting **patterns may link between sculptures**.

### Cyrillic Projector (UNC Charlotte, 1990s)
- **Content:** KGB classified documents about interrogation/psychological control
- **Encryption:** Custom cipher (non-standard)
- **Source:** Text from actual declassified KGB memo + CIA documents from Library of Congress
- **Innovation:** Luminous projection - encrypted text projected onto walls via pinpoint light through perforated cylinder
- **Status:** Solved by amateur cryptographers (Elonka Dunin, Mike Bales, Frank Corr - September 1998)

**KEY FINDING:** Sanborn uses REAL historical documents and ACTUAL intelligence agency materials as plaintext content.

### Ex Nexum (1994-1997) - Little Rock, Arkansas
- **Content:** Greek and Roman writing about historic bankruptcy law changes
- **Design:** S-shaped bronze screen on granite blocks
- **Theme:** Historical/legal significance of location
- **Note:** Less cryptographically complex than Kryptos works

### Lux (2001) - Fort Myers, Florida
- **Design:** Two bronze cylinders (8' high, 5' diameter)
- **Content:** Caloosahatchee Manuscripts (local history)
- **Note:** Text-focused but less emphasis on cipher

### Rippawam (1999) - University of Connecticut, Stamford
- **Content:** Native American texts from Mashpee Petition (1751)
- **Languages:** Algonquin and English translation
- **Method:** Binary conversion + English translation inscribed
- **Theme:** Colonial history and indigenous perspective

**KEY FINDING:** Sanborn's works increasingly focus on **historical documents, local significance, and real-world context** rather than abstract ciphers.

## 10. INTERVIEWS & METHODOLOGY STATEMENTS

### The Smithsonian Oral History (2009)

Jim Sanborn gave a comprehensive 6.5-hour interview to the Smithsonian Institution (Archives of American Art) in July 2009, conducted by art historian Avis Berman. This 70-page, 54,000-word transcript contains significant insights.

**Key Statements:**

**On cryptography's nature:**
> "Cryptography is probably the most closeted of endeavors. It is done in secret. It is done in private. It is just absolutely a private endeavor... It's a very private thing. And cryptographers are notoriously private people who don't have exciting lives."

**On his artistic vision:**
> "They will be able to read what I wrote, but what I wrote is a mystery itself."

**On the creative process:**
> "I never worked with codes in my youth—I was mathematically illiterate."

**On choosing Ed Scheidt:**
> Sanborn "didn't want to invent a cipher based on ancient codes that had already been cracked," so he turned to a CIA veteran who could offer genuine contemporary cryptographic techniques.

### His Advice for K4 Solvers (November 2025)

> "I counsel 'creativity' for anyone looking to find the decipher key to K4."

**What this means:**
- Standard cryptanalysis insufficient
- Unconventional thinking required
- Lateral reasoning important
- Probably some element of intuition or inspired guessing

## 12. THE SMITHSONIAN DISCOVERY (September 2025)

### What Actually Happened

**Journalists Jarett Kobek and Richard Byrne** found documents in Sanborn's donated archive at the Smithsonian Institution.

**The Discovery Process:**
1. Noticed Sanborn's "coding charts" were listed in the Smithsonian's archives
2. Photographed the papers
3. Found **taped-together scraps of text**
4. When assembled, revealed K4's plaintext

**The Plaintext:**
```
UNDERQAPBZDBKZELNORTHEASTLGUWCXDJFQGUZOUAFZFETMMNXPSOZMPAPGKPVHBERLINCLOCKRSPVJWQULABOVEZOLRKCAYF
```

**Why It Was There:**
Sanborn mistakenly included plaintext scraps when compiling his archive during cancer treatment.

### Sanborn's Official Response

> "K4 has not been solved or decrypted. The headlines say they found the 'solution' or the 'key.' These are both very incorrect terms. What they found was scrambled text, and not by solving the cryptogram. They did not solve K4 and they certainly did not find the key."

**Critical Distinction:**
- **Discovered:** The plaintext
- **NOT found:** The encryption method/key
- **Remaining mystery:** How to convert ciphertext OBKRUOXOGHULBSOLIFBBWFLRVQQPRNGKSSOTWTQSJQSSEKZZWATJKLUDIAWINFBNYPVTTMZFPKWGDKZXTJCDIGKUHUAUEKCAR → plaintext

### Archive Sealing

Following the discovery, Sanborn requested the Smithsonian seal the remaining K4-related documents for **50 years**, and they complied.
