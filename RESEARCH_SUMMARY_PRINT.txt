THE BERLIN CLOCK (MENGENLEHREUHR)
================================================================================

What It Is:
- First public clock in world that tells time using colored light fields
- Located in Berlin-Mitte, originally at Kurfürstendamm (near Berlin Wall)
- Designed by Dieter Binninger
- 24-hour display using illuminated colored fields

How It Works (6 rows):
- Row 1: 4 red lights = tens of hours (0-5)
- Row 2: 4 red lights = ones of hours (0-1)
- Row 3: 3 yellow/red lights = tens of minutes (0-5)
- Row 4: 4 yellow lights = ones of minutes (0-1)
- Row 5: 3 red lights = tens of seconds (0-5)
- Row 6: 4 red lights = ones of seconds (0-1)

Historical Significance:
- Gathering place for crowds that tore down Berlin Wall (1989)
- Close to original Wall perimeter
- Conspiracy theory: lights used to signal East Berliners

Cryptographic Possibilities:
1. Time coordinates as cipher key
2. Light patterns as letter values
3. Set theory notation for encryption (clock originally named Mengenlehreuhr 
   = "Set Theory Clock")
4. Positional coordinates of clock location
5. Specific time (Berlin Wall fell ~23:30 on Nov 9, 1989)
